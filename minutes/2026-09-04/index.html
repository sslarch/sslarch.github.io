In memoriam

  • The SIG noted with great sadness the death of Thomas Rose: https://news.mit.edu/2026/mit-assistant-professor-thomas-rose-archaeometallurgy-expert-dies-0903
  • Thomas’ computational archaeology work and projects include:
    • chronoCHRT: https://github.com/archaeothommy/chronochrt
    • ASTR: https://github.com/archaeothommy/ASTR
  • Other contributors will continue the work on these projects, as would have been his wish.

CAA2027

  • The next CAA conference will take place in Santiago, Chile in May 2027: https://2027.caaconference.org
  • The call for papers, posters and workshops is open until October 1 2026: https://2027.caaconference.org/2026/08/31/call-for-papers-posters-and-workshops-open
  • At this stage, few SIG members indicated that they are considering to attend.

SIG SSLA Maintainathon 2027

  • The SIG discussed organising another SIG SSLA Maintainathon: https://sslarch.github.io/maintainathon
  • Possible timing: First or second week of January 2027.
  • The event could potentially involve and reach out to additional groups beyond SIG SSLA:
    • The MAIA COST Action (Managing Artificial Intelligence in Archaeology, https://maiacost.eu) may be a network through which to share information about the Maintainathon. Members of MAIA may also be able and willing to contribute AI-related Maintainathon resources.
    • The SIG will prepare an information package that can be shared with relevant groups and communities.
  • Suggested activities and topics:
    • Blogging and short “speed blog” posts.
    • Small surveys in and beyond the computational archaeology community.
    • Resources and discussions on AI-related topics.
  • Before publicising the event more widely, the Maintainathon website should be reorganised and updated.
  • Zack Batist will write a short blog post summarising the current Maintainathon plans.

SIG SSLA blog and communication

  • The SIG discussed introducing more regular, short blog posts at https://sslarch.github.io/blog.
  • A possible format is “speed blogging”: short posts of approximately ten lines, written collaboratively or quickly around a clearly defined topic.
    • Guidance: https://www.software.ac.uk/guide/speed-blogging-and-tips-writing-speed-blog-post
  • Possible approach: Dedicate part of a SIG meeting every two or three months to drafting and publishing a short post.
  • Possible blog topics:
    • The Digital Archaeology Map.
    • Funding lists and other useful “lists of lists”.
    • Short presentations of their own projects by SIG SSLA members.
    • Domain-specific languages (DSLs) and their growing relevance across research software (following the SIG SSLA session at CAA2025).
    • Small but useful features of software such as RStudio.
    • Guides to accessing data from particular aggregators, such as Euroevol or HYDE.
    • Web scraping examples and good practice, including the importance of checking robots.txt (example: https://lsteinmann.github.io/update/webscraping-packhum-inscriptions).
  • The SIG also discussed its social-media communication:
    • Existing guidelines: https://sslarch.github.io/documents/twitter_guidelines
    • Possible use of automated Mastodon/toot actions.
    • The SIG should improve and more actively maintain responses to questions and interactions on social media.
